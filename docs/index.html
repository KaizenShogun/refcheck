Has anything you cite been quietly corrected?

Paste your bibliography. This tells you which of your references carry a published correction, erratum, expression of concern, withdrawal or retraction — not just the retractions everyone checks for.

Paste a reference list, a BibTeX file, or one DOI per line. Anything works — the DOIs get picked out of the mess. Nothing else is read.

What this is, and what it is not

Retractions are the famous case and free tools already cover them. But a retraction is the rarest kind of change to the scientific record. Corrections and errata are several times more common, and nobody emails you when the figure you built an argument on was corrected two years after you read it — the paper is still perfectly valid, which is exactly why nobody looks.

A change notice is not a verdict on the science. Most corrections are a typo, a wrong unit, a mislabelled axis. This tells you something changed; reading what changed is your job.

It only sees what publishers registered with Crossref. A correction that was never deposited is invisible here, and so is anything without a DOI — preprints, books and chapters often come back “not found”, and this page says so instead of pretending they came back clean.

Your bibliography is not uploaded anywhere

There is no server behind this page. Your text stays in your browser; only the DOIs found in it are sent, directly from your browser to Crossref’s public API. No account, no cookies, no analytics, nothing logged by me. You can save this page and run it offline from your own disk — it is one HTML file with no dependencies.
